# 03 — Customer and Pricing Reassessment

**Basis:** `appendix/p3_a2_reachability_trust_pricing.md` (30-row register; all external claims are search snippets, mostly vendor-sourced), `appendix/p3_a4_customer_evidence.md` (22 confirmed URLs, **0 bodies read**), `appendix/p3_a5_skeptic_review.md`, Phase 2 files `03`, `06`, `07`. **No interviews were conducted; no customer demand is claimed.** All probabilities below are my assumptions unless a source is named.

## 1. Phase 2's customer and price plan, restated
Shopify + QuickBooks businesses with a wholesale channel, $2–20M revenue; manual "Profit & Cash Leak Audit" at $500/$1,000/$1,500 paid before results; 15 qualified prospects; success = at least 4 payers in 30 days.

## 2. Can we realistically identify 15 qualified businesses?

| What does that imply? | The skeptic's illustrative funnel (assumptions): pessimistic 300 touches × 3% reply × 35% qualified ≈ **3**; base 400 × 6% × 45% ≈ **11**; optimistic 400 × 10% × 60% ≈ **24**. A zero-audience founder should expect **5–12 qualified conversations** in three weeks, not 15 | Assumption |

**Answer:** 15 qualified conversations is *plausible with heavy manual outreach* (hundreds of touches). It is not an inbound result.

## 3. Can we reach decision-makers, and through which channels?

- **Communities cannot be the lead channel.** Shopify Community guidelines bar promotion and promotional private messages (primary guidelines, seen via snippet). r/shopify and r/ecommerce rules say much the same (third-party summaries and mirrors only; Reddit could not be read). Use them to listen and build credibility, never to pitch.
- **What remains:** hand-built manual outbound to owners; introductions from accountants, ecommerce bookkeepers and fractional CFOs; existing network; trade associations and ERP resellers for distributors.
- **Inbound reality:** a landing page and a free tool convert only when traffic exists. No measured keyword demand exists for the target phrases (no keyword tool was accessible). Inbound is a *credibility asset* in this plan, not a lead engine.
- **Timing:** today is 2026-11-19. Black Friday/Cyber Monday and December year-end are the worst weeks to ask an ecommerce owner or their bookkeeper for exports and a decision. January is better (year-end review is a natural trigger), but US accountants get busy with tax season from February — whether that applies to ecommerce-specialist advisors is unverified.

## 4. Why would an unknown company be trusted with financial data?

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Surveys are dated and mostly non-US: Xero (2021) only 39% understood how third parties access their data; UK FSB 15% of small firms share bank data electronically; Codat 67% of potential borrowers would share with credit suppliers; AAPI 58% willing for financing | Willingness is **conditional on a concrete benefit and on who is asking**. No survey tests an unknown US consultant |
| QuickBooks read-only for an invited accountant: an Intuit Community thread (undated) says the option is not available and invited accountants get admin-level access; a vendor blog says limited access is possible but unclear; snippets conflict | If true, "invited read-only user" becomes "admin access for a stranger" — a much harder ask. **Use exports, not logins** |
| SOC 2: demanded mainly by mid-size/enterprise buyers; small buyers tolerate small vendors (compliance vendors and one practitioner post) | Unlikely to be the barrier at $500–$2,000. References and an accountant's endorsement are more plausible, but nothing ranks them |
| Customer-voice evidence | Phase 3 found snippets only: QuickBooks users reportedly distrust Intuit Assist, ask for an off switch and re-check its output (cuts both ways for a third-party explainer); developers on Hacker News wire Claude to QuickBooks themselves and refuse to give agents write access. **No bodies read** |

**Trust mitigations that are cheap and credible:** exports only; read-only; a one-page written data-handling note (what is collected, where it lives, deletion within X days on request, no training on customer data); named humans; the first scan free and bounded so trust is built before money moves; an accountant's introduction where possible.

## 5. Why would they pay before seeing results?

**Mostly they would not, from an unknown seller.** Evidence:
- Recovery-style audits (parcel, Amazon FBA) are sold on **contingency or free-first** (parcel audit 25–50% of recovered refunds; Amazon reimbursement 10–30%; some "free audit, pay on recovery"). Prepaying for a finding of unknown size is the opposite of the norm in those categories.
- Marketing/ecommerce agencies commonly sell **free store audits as lead magnets** and charge for the fix. One source warns that a free diagnosis can leave the incumbent provider to get paid for the remedy.
- Productized-audit advice: label it an "audit" with a named deliverable; credit the fee toward the main programme; "you keep the output".
- **No controlled comparison of prepaid vs free-first conversion was found.**
- Interpretation problem: a *failure* of a pay-first test is ambiguous (no demand, no trust, no timing, or no brand). Only a design that separates trust from value can interpret a failure.

## 6. Is $500–$1,500 realistic?

| What owners already pay (vendor-published) | Price |
|---|---|
| Bookkeeper (ecommerce) | from ≈ $250/mo; bookkeeping-led tiers $995–$3,500/mo |
| Profit apps | $19–$720/mo ($228–$8,640/yr) |
| Fractional CFO | $3,000–$12,000/mo; light retainers lower |
| Published fixed-price ecommerce diagnostic (Eightx "Growth Economics Audit") | **starts at $5,000** one-time (page conflicts with others that say pricing is set on a call) |
| Shopify agency audits (conversion/growth/SEO) | $997 flat; $1,500 (7-day); $1,950 (ten-business-day) — marketing, not profit |
| Bookkeeping cleanup | $249 one-time (Webgility) to ≈ $3,000–$9,000 CAD (catch-up); general guides $500–$5,000+ |
| Free audits | widely offered by agencies as lead magnets |

**Reading:** the *number* $500–$1,500 is inside what owners pay agencies for a one-off audit. What is unusual is not the price but the **risk asymmetry**: unknown seller, prepayment, financial data, unknown size of the finding, no references, and the owner's bookkeeper having to pull exports. At $500–$1,000 each audit also loses money against 8–28 hours of delivery at $55/hour ($440–$1,540) before founder time — it is a test cost, not an offset.

**Would a smaller paid diagnostic be more realistic?** Yes, for *a narrow, self-contained deliverable* ($99–$300 precedents: cleanup from $249; many $997 audits). A $149–$299 "data review" (buyer uploads exports; seller returns a short list of anomalies and what could not be checked) shrinks the risk and the security ask and tests whether *anyone* pays — but it does not test the $400+/month economics. The cleaner structure is a **free, capped, scripted scan followed by a paid investigation** (see `04`).

## 7. What is the actual alternative they pay for today?
Ranked by how directly it competes (all vendor-sourced prices):
1. **Nothing / the owner and a spreadsheet** (most common per forum posts and Phase 1 surveys).
2. **Bookkeeper or ecommerce accountant** who produces monthly P&L and, when asked, a margin explanation.
3. **A profit app** ($19–$720/mo) for visibility.
4. **A fractional CFO** ($3–12K/mo) for diagnosis and decisions.
5. **A rented AI** (Claude with connectors, Intuit/Xero AI, Sidekick) at roughly a licence fee — documented, free connectors, unknown depth.
A product priced at $349+/month has to beat (5) on evidence quality and (2)–(4) on price. That is the real competitive frame.

## 8. What would make them purchase immediately?
Evidence is thin and mostly services marketing:
- A **specific trigger** (supplier cost change, wholesale launch, margin drop, lender/investor request, year-end close). Only supplier cost shocks (2025) are sourced; the rest are assumptions.
- A **tight named deliverable and timeline**.
- **Risk reversal:** keep-the-output; credit toward the next step; refund for non-delivery; contingency where the finding is a recoverable dollar amount.
- **A warm introduction** from their own accountant or fractional CFO — the only plausible cure for the trust gap, and unproven for non-accounting software.
- **Social proof:** none exists yet.

## 9. Honest probability assessment (qualitative; no sourced rates exist)
- **15 qualified conversations in 30 days:** plausible only with heavy manual outreach; likely 5–12 for a zero-audience team in three weeks; worse in late November–December.
- **4 prepaying strangers out of 15:** **low-likelihood.** A good product (30% true conversion) would still miss the pass line 30% of the time; at 20% it passes 35%.
- **A result of 0–2 payers:** uninterpretable unless every loss is coded (no reply / not the right stack / no wholesale / no urgency / would not share data / would not prepay / price / wanted free) and a free or low-risk arm exists to separate trust from value.
- **Failure modes of pay-first:** selection (only friends pay); founder-fit (engineers selling finance with no domain credibility); procurement friction (bookkeeper gatekeeper; card before an NDA); data friction (exports lack product cost; cleaning costs more than the fee); refund/liability exposure; signal contamination (discounting or accepting friends to hit a number); community bans; Q4 seasonality; competitors offering free audits as lead magnets.

## 10. Reassessed numbers for the redesigned experiment

| Phase 2 | Phase 3 recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 15 qualified prospects, one pooled cohort | **Up to 25 qualified scan recipients, two cohorts reported separately**; report "not run" (not "fail") if < 10 qualify in six weeks | Power, heterogeneity, reach |
| ≥ 4 payers of 15 (26.7%) | **Sequential test on paid conversion** among qualified recipients (H0 10% vs H1 30%): stop for success when 1.10·payers − 0.25·non-payers ≥ 2.20 (e.g., 2 payers and 0 non-payers; 3 payers and ≤ 4 non-payers); stop for failure when 0.25·non-payers − 1.10·payers ≥ 2.20 (0 payers in 9; 1 in 14; 2 in 18) | Better error rates at similar sample; stops early |
| $500/$1,000/$1,500 tiers | **One price per cohort:** $750 (Shopify cohort), $2,000 (distributor cohort, introductions only) | Three tiers over ≈11 prospects give 3–4 per cell: no price signal readable |
| Pay before seeing anything | **Free, capped, scripted scan first; pay for the investigation** | Separates trust from value; avoids the unknown-seller prepay wall |
| 30 days starting now | **Gate 0 and desk work now; customer-facing work from early January; ≈ 8–10 weeks elapsed** | Q4 seasonality; reachability |
| Accountant pilots free and counted | **Accountant arm is qualitative only; never counted toward the payer rule** | Free arms cannot measure willingness to pay |
| 120–150 founder hours | **≤ 120 hours hard cap** (Gate 0 ≤ 25, Stage 1 ≤ 95 including scripts, list and ≈3 investigations); bottom-up estimate of the Phase 2 design was 127–207 | Honest hours |
