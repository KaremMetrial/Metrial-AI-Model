# P3-A3 — Evidence Audit of Phase 2 Claims

Auditor: Phase-3 Agent 3 (evidence auditor). Audit date: 2026-11-19. Scope: 48 high-impact claims taken from Phase 2 (`research/phase2/00`, `02`, `03`, `04`, `06`, `08`, appendices `p2_a1`, `p2_a3a`, `p2_a3b`, `p2_a4`, `p2_a5`, and the source register `10`) plus Phase 1 appendices where Phase 2 inherited a claim. Read-only: no one contacted, nothing purchased, no code written.

Decision under review: **do not build yet; run a 30-day paid audit; margin investigation is the lead hypothesis.**

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Nothing was verified from a full page.** Every WebFetch attempt (15 hosts) failed with `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`; this session's network allowlist does not include the publishers. All checks below were made on WebSearch result summaries, so the best status I can give is **VERIFIED-SNIPPET**. I did not mark any row VERIFIED-FULL-PAGE.
2. **Most Phase 2 numbers exist and are quoted correctly.** Of 48 claims, 31 are VERIFIED-SNIPPET, 13 are PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (two of them not supported as worded), 3 are UNVERIFIABLE with this tooling, and 1 is mixed (see Section 3.6). The Phase 2 authors were mostly honest about sourcing quality.
3. **The numbers are mostly right; the inferences drawn from them are where the risk sits.** The ten most dangerous overstatements (Section 4) are about interpretation: using 91%/45%/45% as evidence that "behavior contradicts the premise", applying a $100M-$1B mid-cap leakage estimate to a $10-75M ICP, treating one aggregator-relayed figure about Rebel Cheese as proof that recoveries "decay", and treating partner-blog claims about NetSuite Ask Oracle as proof that an ERP-native substitute exists.
4. **The decision survives.** None of the corrections reverses "do not build yet; run a paid audit". Several corrections weaken the evidence for *margin* as the lead hypothesis (the dollar-impact figures are mismatched to the ICP and sponsor-authored; the frequency evidence is stale). Several others *strengthen* the platform-risk case (Xero's AI-training ban is confirmed by Xero's own FAQ, not merely "reported"). Wording in the Phase 2 decision documents should be adjusted as listed in Section 5.

Status legend: **VF** = VERIFIED-FULL-PAGE (none), **VS** = VERIFIED-SNIPPET (seen in a search summary of the named source or a close mirror), **PS** = PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, **NS** = NOT SUPPORTED, **CX** = CONTRADICTED, **UV** = UNVERIFIABLE with available tooling. Impact = effect on the decision (H/M/L).

## 4. The ten most dangerous overstatements

Ranked by how much each could mislead the committee on the three-part decision.

**1. "The behavior contradicts the premise" (00, Section on why not to build; repeated in 03 and 08).** Phase 2 reads "91% raise prices" (Baird-NAW 2Q26) plus "45% pass through all, 45% most" (DSG Q4 2026) as evidence that distributors already pass costs through. The data cannot carry that. 91% is a multi-select "do you raise prices in response to rising COGS" item, not a measure of how much, how fast, or on which SKUs. The 45%/45% item means that **45% of distributors do not pass through all supplier increases**, which is what a leakage hypothesis predicts. The MDM article's own headline says costs changed in three places while pricing changed once. Phase 2 does admit the article body was unread, but still ranks this as one of three reasons not to proceed. Correct to: "these surveys neither confirm nor refute leakage; they measure intent to raise prices, not realised pass-through."

**2. 100-300 bps and "3-5% revenue leak" as the size of the prize for a $10-75M ICP (00, 02).** The 100-300 bps estimate is from an article about **$100M-$1B** distributors, written by DSG with a pricing-services vendor, no methodology. The 3-5% figure comes from a report co-sponsored by a consulting firm whose landing page sells "Add 3-5% to your annual revenue", and its own numbers do not reconcile (3-5% of $50M is $1.5-2.5M, but the report's example is $750K-$1M, equal to 150-200 bps). "Revenue leak" is the wrong label. Phase 2 does call these "sponsor-biased", but still places them as the only sizing evidence for the lead hypothesis. Correct to: "no independent evidence of leakage size for $10-75M distributors exists; sponsor estimates for larger firms are 1.5-3 points."

**3. "Recoveries decay: a company built its own audit agent and recoveries fell from $400K to below $40K" (00, 02, p2_a3b).** The $400K is a cumulative one-year saving in Fortune; the "<$40K" is relayed by aggregators with no period, and one says "$40K a month", which annualises above $400K. Phase 2 quoted a causal reason in quotation marks (carriers "now knowing the tool catches overcharges") that I could not find in any summary. The inference "recovery products are decaying" supports dismissing the strongest proven-payment category. Correct to: "one company reports lower recent savings; period and cause unconfirmed; DIY with general AI is a real substitute."

**4. "NetSuite Ask Oracle ('why did margins drop') exists" listed among substitutes (00, 01, 02, 08).** Only partner and consultancy blogs describe it; one says it is in preview; Oracle documentation was not found. Phase 2 flags "accuracy untested" but not availability. It is also NetSuite-only. Correct to: "partner-advertised, availability unconfirmed, irrelevant outside NetSuite".

**5. Margin pain "frequency: High, monthly/weekly" in the opportunity matrix (02, margin row).** Supported only by a spring-2025 survey of expected tariff cost increases (A01-A02), which measures cost shocks, not *unexplained* margin erosion. Phase 2's own text (00 item 1) says unexplained-erosion frequency is unknown, but the 02 matrix presents "High" as a cell value next to sourced numbers. Also the tariff trigger may fade (03 says so). Correct to: "frequency of cost shock: high in 2025; frequency of unexplained erosion: unknown."

**6. Cost and timing assumptions placed in the same column as sourced facts (02, 04).** "3-5 days" to trusted first insight, "1-2 days" for cash/AR, "4-16 h cost cleanup", and "unitCost current-value only" appear in the "data feasibility" column next to platform facts. The first three are assumptions (02 marks one "(assumption)"; the others are not marked). The last is forum-based (A15). They drive the margin vs cash ranking. Correct: tag every timing cell [A] and run the 10-merchant cost-completeness sample before using them.

**7. "Small firms invest less in pricing tools (27% vs 55% at $500M+)" (03, 08).** The split was not found in any Simon-Kucher summary, the "$500M+" threshold is unconfirmed, and 27% is also the share ranking pricing AI first (A10), a different survey, and the share of SMB Group financial decision-makers who are owners (27%), and the 4-of-15 conversion rate (about 27%) in 06. Four unrelated 27%s in one evidence set invite mix-ups. Correct: cite only after reading the gated report.

**8. "Reach is real (94% of US accounting firms offer advisory)" (00, 03, 08).** The figure is correct (A34) but is a vendor-panel statement that firms offer something called advisory. It does not show that firms recommend or resell non-accounting tools; 03 itself says no evidence exists on that. Correct: "94% claim advisory; resale behaviour unknown."

**9. "Nobody in any researched category has been shown to pay for an explanation product" (00, 01).** This is an absence-of-evidence claim made while G2, Reddit, apps.shopify.com, help.shopify.com, netsuite.com and most primary host pages were blocked (Phase 2's own caveat). Zenlytic, Hypersonix, Polar and Lifetimely were not trialled. The statement may be true but cannot be tested with this evidence. Correct: "I found no evidence; coverage was incomplete (listed hosts blocked)."

**10. Platform-term reliability inverted in places (00, 03, 07, 08).** Phase 2 repeatedly says Xero's AI-training ban is "reportedly... single search summary; verify". Evidence now includes Xero's own FAQ, The Register and Accounting Today (A22). Similarly Merge pricing is labelled "competitor-authored" though Merge publishes it (A31) and Fathom's price is called "provisional pre-production" though the official page matches (A35). This is under-claiming, but under-claiming here makes the "verify" backlog look larger and the moat risk look softer than it is. Correct as in rows A22, A31, A35. (The Xero point actually raises the severity of the moat risk.)

Honourable mentions: Techaisle used as "the only sourced anchor" though undated and likely 2018-2021 (A38); Shopify "4+ months" review time from one thread title (A18); "Intuit Intelligence ... reconciliation explanations" when sources describe flagging (A27).

## 8. Limits of this audit

- No full page was read. Every status is bounded by the quality of a search summary, which can drop caveats (sample, base, period) or, rarely, mis-state them. I tried to flag where a summary itself said it could not confirm a figure.

- I audited 48 claims; about 170 register rows were not individually tested. The untested rows are mostly lower-impact (competitor features, reviews, forum anecdotes).

- Next step to convert statuses to VERIFIED-FULL-PAGE: allowlist mdm.com, naw.org, distributionstrategy.com, vendavo.com, shopify.dev, developer.xero.com, developer.intuit.com, fortune.com, wolterskluwer.com, and read A03/A05/A06/A07/A12/A15/A22/A44 first; those eight carry most of the decision weight.
